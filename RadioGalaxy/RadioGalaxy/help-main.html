The RadioGalaxy player lets you search for and play Internet Radio stations. Once a station is selected, it begins streaming, and by default keeps streaming even if you switch to listen to another station. When you return to the previous station, playback resumes from the point where you left off.

If you want streaming to stop when you switch away from a station, you can either press the Stop icon (the black box), or go to the More.../Settings menu and turn off 'Stream even after switch.'

Basic Operation

There are four control buttons at the bottom of the screen. The "-20" button moves back 20 seconds in the playing stream, and "+20" moves forward 20 seconds. The Play/Pause button pauses or resumes the current station, although the station keeps streaming even when playing is paused. The Stop (square) button stops both the player and the stream.

Pressing a station's icon will play or resume the station. The icon colors and animation tells you the current state:

  • Purple+Flashing
    • Currently playing radio station and streaming data.
  • Orange
    • Last playing radio station, but the station is no longer playing or streaming data. Pressing the 'play' button will resume streaming and playing audio.
  • Blue+Flashing
    • Not playing, but still streaming audio in the background.
  • Green
    • Idle station: neither playing nor streaming data.

A long press on any station's icon will display details about the station, including its name, streaming URL and current transfer rate. The details page also contains an "Edit Station" button that let's you change any of the station parameters, or to remove the station entirely.

From this details page, you can immediately stop its streaming, erase all the streamed music for the station, snapshot the contents of a station's downloaded music or freeze downloads for that station.

Creating a snapshot creates a new station icon with a copy of the station's already streamed music. No further changes are made to the station -- no music is garbage collected no matterhowold, nor are any downloads started again. You can however select the station by pressing its icon and see around with the scroll bar.

A frozen station is one where no more downloads or garbage ccollection occurs. It can be unfrozen by toggling the freeze station button again.

The Highlight button on the main screen tells RadioGalaxy to highlight the currently playing song in the history list so you can later review music you found interesting.

Adding and Editing Station Details

The first menu item in the Main Menu (the green button on the screen) is "+Station." If pressed, you can search for stations by name or keywords. The search page has its own help pages that describe in detail how to search for and add radio stations, but you can search by name, keyword, country code, city name and/or genre. Note that not all of the station directories distinguish between searching for keywords or searching for titles.

Once the search has populated candidate stations into the visible table view, just select the stations you want to add to RadioGalaxy's home page by pressing on the station's table entry; a check mark will be visible in the table.

If you swipe left on a validated station, you can listen to a sample of the station's stream.

If you long press a station's icon and then press the "Edit Station" button, you can edit the station's parameters, including its name, description and the station's stream URL There are also buttons to cancel the update, end the editing session and to remove the station from RadioGalaxy entirely.

Streaming Control

The Settings page has a button that enables or disables streaming in the background. If enabled (the default) the station will keep streaming data after you switch away to listen to another station, and will resume playing where you left off when you return to the original station.

If this is disabled, the stream will stop if you switch to play another station, and will resume from the latest point in the stream when selected again. However, you can still use the "-20" button or slider to play already streamed data from the station.

You can set this behavior separately for switching from the app's main screen, or when switching from the lock screen or CarPlay controls.

Menu Operations

If you press the "More..." menu button under the station icons, it pops up a list of functions RadioGalaxy can perform.

  • Manually add station
    • If a station is missing from all of our databases, you can still add a station in manually as long as you know its stream URL.
  • Settings
    • Adjust settings like how much streamed music to keep. See settings section below.
  • Help
    • Displays this text.
  • Show history
    • RadioGalaxy tracks each song played on a station, and logs it in a history file. You can highlight songs to remember by swiping on the entry. When you erase all the songs in the history, all the highlighted songs are preserved untouched, although you can manually remove highlighted songs by removing the highlight or delete them on a song by song basis.
  • Legal
    • RadioGalaxy makes use of some directory services that require acknowledgement or other legal statements, and those are listed here.

Settings

There are several settings for RadioGalaxy
  • Stream even after switch
    • When enabled, tells RadioGalaxy to keep streaming a station even after you switch away from it. If not enabled, switching away from a station stops downloading music from that station. Defaults to enabled.
  • Stream even after CarPlay
    • Keep streaming after switching to 'Next' or 'Prev' via CarPlay or the Lock Screen.
  • Keep 2.5 hours of audio
    • Specifies in 0.5 hour increments how long an audio recording is kept for each station.
  • Limit search to N stations
    • Limit each directory to returning no more than N stations.
  • Animate streaming stations
    • The icons for currently streaming stations fade in and out if this setting is enabled. This feature burns through battery while the screen is visible.

Exporting Music

You can export music recorded by a station using the Export menu item. Some stations label each song played with ICY metadata, and for those stations, you can simply press the 'Add all recorded songs' button and all the songs present will appear in the table at the top of the Export screen. You can then simply swipe left on the item and press 'Export' and the recording will be saved. You can also press 'Play' and listen to the entry. Or you can press 'Update times' and adjust the start and end times for the recording.

For stations that don't export the song metadata, you can use the 'Start' and 'End' sliders to select a time range from the streamed data to save. When you move the 'Start' slider, you'll hear the 5 seconds of audio starting at the selected time. When you move the 'End' slider you'll hear the last 5 seconds ending at the selected time. Once you've selected the desired range, press the 'Add from range' button, and you'll be asked for the song name for the recording, and then the audio will be saved.

Some stations export ICY metadata, but don't position the metadata correctly: the metadata is only approximately positioned. For these stations, you can select the song you wish to export by tapping the entry in the table. RadioGalaxy will highlight the entry and adjust the start and end scrollbar, which you can adjust with the scroll bars as described above. You can then swipe left on the entry and select 'Update times' and the start and end times will be adjusted to match the scroll bars. Finally, you can select 'Export' from the swiped entry and RadioGalaxy will export the audio into a file named by the ICY metadata. Alternatively, after adjusting the start and end scroll bars, you can just press 'Add from range' and enter the song name again; this will also add a new entry with the updated times to the end of the table.

My apologies for the clunky user interface.

Accessing exported files

To access your exported files, plug your iPhone or iPad into a Mac, and use the Finder to select the phone from the locations on the left. When the phone's details show up in the Finder, select 'Files' from the Finder screen, and then scroll down to RadioGalaxy's entry and click on the arrow to expand it. You can then drag and drop the file to your Mac's home directory and then add it to your library with the Music app.

Noet that once you've expanded the files for RadioGalaxy, the Mac Finder is too stupid to notice further updates without your unplugging and re-plugging the phone in with a USB cable.

Note that stations that stream MP3 data will simply write MP3 files with the right metadata (but no album artwork, and probably no album name). But stations that stream AAC data will write AAC files with the appropriate metadata. You can play those with VLC, but if you want to add them to your music library, you should convert them to M4A files. The easiest way to do that is to use ffmpeg, which can be downloaded with Homebrew. You can then do: 

	ffmpeg -i foo.aac -c:a foo.m4a
to convert the file from a pure AAC file to an M4A, preserving the song and album metadata written by RadioGalaxy. Hopefully I will get time to implement an M4A writer, but it's a bit of pain.
